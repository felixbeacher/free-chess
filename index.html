How Free Chess works
  1. Build your opening. White uses ranks 1–4; Black uses ranks 5–8. Place your usual 16 pieces on any empty squares in your half. Tap a piece in the tray, then a square. Tap a placed piece to move it; tap it again to return it to the tray.
  2. Choose or randomise. Choose your own positions, or generate a random formation and reshuffle it before playing. You are White; the computer is Black. Its formation is generated independently before your setup and stays hidden until you start.
  3. Play normal chess. White moves first. Legal moves, check, checkmate, stalemate, promotion and en passant are enforced. Pawns move toward the other side and may move two squares only from their usual starting rank.
  4. Castling. A king must start on e1/e8 and its rook on a1/h1/a8/h8. The usual movement, empty-square and check restrictions apply.
  5. A bold opening has risks. Your hidden arrangement may start in check, or even checkmate. White must resolve any check on its first turn; Black must resolve any remaining check on its turn. Kings are never capturable.
  6. Draws. Claim a draw after three repetitions or 50 moves by each side without a pawn move or capture. Five repetitions, 75 such moves, stalemate and standard insufficient-material positions end the game automatically. The computer claims available draws on its turn. It may accept your draw offer in an approximately balanced position after move 10.

The computer is a casual opponent that thinks a few moves ahead. Everything runs in your browser, with no account, downloads or connection needed. Reloading this page clears the game.
